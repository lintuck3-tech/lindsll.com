Graduate capstone · Research synthesis

Interventions to Improve Mental Health Amongst Foster Youth.

A comprehensive literature review comparing trauma focused interventions for foster youth experiencing PTSD and the service gaps that shape whether care is actually accessible.

Lindsey Tuck & Kimberlee Duncan-Rodriguez · San Francisco State University · 2021

01

The question

Foster youth experience trauma and PTSD at disproportionately high rates, yet choosing an intervention is only part of the challenge. This capstone asked which evidence-informed treatments successfully reduce PTSD symptoms, what makes them work, and where gaps in services prevent young people from benefiting.

02

The approach

We conducted a comprehensive review of 52 peer-reviewed articles published between 2000 and 2021. Using social-science and psychology databases, we compared study populations, methods, outcomes, and limitations across five interventions selected for their evidence base and relevance to child welfare.

The analysis paired evidence of effectiveness with a second question: can foster youth realistically access and complete the treatment?

03

What we found

Every treatment showed value, but no single model was the universal answer. Fit depended on a young person’s age, symptoms, caregiver involvement, placement stability, delivery setting, and access to a trained provider.

01

PCIT

Strengthens caregiver-child relationships and addresses disruptive behavior.

02

TF-CBT

Addresses trauma symptoms, shame, and distorted beliefs with caregiver support.

03

CPP

Supports young children and caregivers after trauma exposure.

04

PE-A

Uses structured exposure to reduce PTSD symptoms in adolescents.

05

EMDR

Helps process traumatic memories and reduce PTSD, anxiety, and fear.

04

Why access matters

Barriers appeared at every level: language and cultural fit for individual families; placement changes, advocacy gaps, and provider coordination across agencies; and fragmented systems, reimbursement limits, and shortages of trained clinicians. The review showed that continuity and coordination are not side issues. They influence whether an evidence-based treatment can work at all.

MicroLanguage, culture, trust, fit
MezzoPlacement, continuity, coordination
MacroFunding, coverage, provider access

05

What this project demonstrates

The capstone brought together clinical evidence and systems-level thinking. It required turning a large research base into a useful comparison, identifying patterns across imperfect information, and keeping the practical needs of children, caregivers, and practitioners at the center.

  • Research synthesis
  • Comparative analysis
  • Trauma-informed practice
  • Policy & systems thinking
  • Collaborative writing

A note on scope: The review focused on five selected interventions. Similar symptoms, co-occurring needs, and limited long-term research make it difficult to attribute every outcome to one treatment or generalize beyond foster youth.

End of project

Evidence matters. Access and context do, too.
