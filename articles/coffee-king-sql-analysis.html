SQL case study · Nashville Market Opportunity Analysis

Coffee King.

Using five Yelp tables and 817,283 Nashville check-ins, I built a SQL decision model to compare foot traffic, coffee-shop supply, opening hours, and amenities across Metro planning areas. The analysis identified Donelson–Hermitage–Old Hickory as the strongest place to begin a site search and pointed to an early-morning, parking-friendly concept.

01

The business question

I wanted to know where a new Nashville coffee shop should begin looking, when existing hours might leave a service gap, and which amenities were associated with stronger engagement. Because Yelp does not measure unmet coffee demand directly, I treated the project as a way to narrow the search rather than make a final site decision.

02

How I approached it

I joined five Yelp tables covering 6,971 Nashville businesses. Yelp categories initially identified 368 possible coffee businesses. I used business names and coffee-related review language to narrow that group to 225, then removed shops marked closed, leaving 151 open coffee shops for the analysis.

I mapped businesses to Metro planning areas, separated 817,283 check-in timestamps into individual events, and compared each area’s share of business activity with its share of coffee shops. I also compared coffee-shop check-ins with recorded opening hours and tested whether amenities were associated with differences in ratings, reviews, and check-ins.

I used Hex’s AI agent to help draft and troubleshoot queries. I set the analytical logic, reviewed the SQL and outputs, and refined the analysis before using the results.

The goal was not just to rank neighborhoods. It was to turn messy, imperfect data into a decision someone could act on.

03

Where to open

Donelson–Hermitage–Old Hickory ranked first in the initial screen. Its 588 businesses, 8 coffee shops, and 7,022 check-ins per shop produce the strongest foot-traffic-to-coffee-supply balance in the county. Its 1.30 opportunity index means the area’s share of citywide check-ins is 1.3 times its share of Nashville’s coffee shops. The lead is meaningful, but narrow enough to treat as a screening signal rather than proof of unmet demand.

04

When to open

Among the 145 coffee shops with usable hours, the median schedule ran from 7am to 6pm. Coffee-shop check-in activity rose sharply toward its 8 to 10am peak, while only 76% of weekday shops were open at 7am. Check-in activity stayed ahead of recorded open-hour coverage from 6am through 11am, with the widest gap at 9am.

05

What to offer

Amenities were more closely associated with engagement than with star ratings. Among shops with a recorded value, those reporting bike parking had 136% more median reviews and 117% more median check-ins than those reporting no bike parking. Shops reporting a parking lot had 171% more median check-ins, the largest observed difference in the analysis.

These findings show association, not causation. Busier shops may also maintain more complete Yelp profiles.

06

Recommendation & limits

01

Evidence

A 1.30 opportunity index, an early-morning coverage gap, and the strongest amenity differences around parking.

02

Decision

I would prioritize Donelson–Hermitage–Old Hickory for more detailed site research, explore an opening time around 6am, and favor properties with convenient parking.

03

Validate

Before selecting a location, I would compare current rent, traffic, competitors, and neighborhood demographics.

Read with care

Yelp check-ins span 2010 through January 2022 and measure historical business activity rather than coffee demand alone. The analysis also compares shops marked open in the January 2022 snapshot with traffic accumulated over the full historical period. The findings should narrow a site search, not make the final decision.
