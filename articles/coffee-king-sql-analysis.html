Coffee King.

01

The business question

Where in Nashville should a new coffee shop open to meet the strongest demand? And once it opens, which hours and amenities are most likely to help it succeed? I treated those as connected decisions rather than three separate analyses.

02

How I approached it

I used SQL in Hex to explore five related Yelp tables covering 6,971 Nashville businesses. I classified genuine coffee shops using categories, business names, and coffee language in reviews; mapped them to Metro planning areas; then compared supply with 817,283 check-ins, opening hours, and reported amenities.

The goal was not just to rank neighborhoods. It was to turn messy, imperfect data into a decision someone could act on.

03

Where to open

Donelson–Hermitage–Old Hickory screens best. Its 588 businesses, 8 coffee shops, and 7,022 check-ins per shop produce the strongest foot-traffic-to-coffee-supply balance in the county. The lead is meaningful, but narrow enough to treat as a screening signal rather than proof of unmet demand.

04

When to open

Coffee demand peaks once, between 8 and 10am, while the median shop does not open until 7am and coverage does not peak until 11am. Demand runs ahead of open-hour supply from 6 to 11am, with the widest gap at 9am.

Demand Open-hour supply

Early hours are the opening. King Coffee can differentiate before much of the existing market is fully online.

05

What to offer

Amenities relate to engagement much more than ratings. Parking is the clearest recurring signal, although the relationship should not be read as causal because high-traffic businesses may also maintain more complete Yelp profiles.

06

Recommendation & limits

Recommendation

  • Prioritize Donelson–Hermitage–Old Hickory.
  • Open early to capture the 6–11am demand window.
  • Favor sites with convenient parking.
  • Validate with current rent, traffic, and demographics.

Read with care

Yelp check-ins span 2010 through January 2022 and measure general business traffic, not coffee demand alone. The index is a strong place to start a site search, not the last word on one.
