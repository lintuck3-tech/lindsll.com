01

Project overview

NCMEC publishes annual data about CyberTipline reports involving suspected child sexual abuse material, online enticement, child sex trafficking, and other forms of child sexual exploitation. I organized the 2025 public tables into one dataset and used Hex to examine how reports were submitted and where records were routed for possible response.

The goal was to bring a new perspective to NCMEC's public data while being clear about what the data can and cannot show.

02

Data & approach

I combined NCMEC's public reporting tables with U.S. Census Bureau population estimates for states and World Bank population estimates for countries. I organized the reporting, provider, state, and country tables and checked the results against NCMEC's published totals before analyzing them in Hex.

What I analyzed

I focused on three questions:

Reporting

Providers

How did state and country routing patterns change after considering population size?

05

How to read it

State figures count records routed to a known state or D.C. Country figures count records routed or made available worldwide. Neither represents unique victims, confirmed offenses, or abuse prevalence, and population-adjusted routing rates are not victimization rates.

06

What it demonstrates

This project brings together my child-welfare experience with practical data analysis. I used SQL and Hex to organize public NCMEC data, compare reporting and routing patterns, and explain the findings in a clear and responsible way. Future analysis will compare multiple years of CyberTipline data to see whether reporting and routing patterns persist over time.
